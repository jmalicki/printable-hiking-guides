Why not just drive to Muir Woods?

You can, and nearly a million people a year do. But the famous grove is the bottom of a much bigger, much emptier forest, and the best way to see it is to arrive on foot from above. Same redwoods, a completely different day.

The front door
  • A parking or shuttle reservation is always required, for a timed slot, booked ahead on gomuirwoods.com. Popular days sell out.
  • A winding drive to the valley floor, then a paved path and boardwalk shared with tour-bus groups.
  • Most visitors walk the 1.5 km loop, take the photo and leave within the hour.
  • You see one thing: big trees, from a boardwalk.
The back door — this hike
  • No reservation. Park at the trailhead opposite the Inn, or take the bus that stops at its door.
  • Two hours of quiet trail first: ridge views to the Pacific, mossy Douglas-fir forest, then a fern canyon beside a tumbling creek.
  • You enter the old growth at its quiet upstream end and walk down through Cathedral Grove — the same celebrated trees, reached on your own legs.
  • It ends at a deck above the fog with a drink in your hand.

The honest trade-off: it costs you a morning rather than an hour, and the redwoods are at the bottom, so every metre you walk down you must walk back up. Read the next section before you commit.

Is this for me? An honest fitness check

If you can jog 5 km without stopping, or cycle 40–50 km at touring pace, your heart and lungs will be fine. Your legs are another matter: nothing in the Netherlands prepares them for this much down and up.

Overall effortAbout the same as a brisk 13 km walk on the flat (rule of thumb: every 100 m of climbing costs as much as an extra kilometre). That is 2½ hours of actual walking, plus stops.
The total climbRoughly 350 m — more than walking from sea level to the top of the Vaalserberg (322 m). Most of it comes in one piece, at the end.
The descent (km 3–5)250 m down in 2 km on the Bootjack Trail: uneven log steps, roots and rock, an average 12% gradient. Cyclists and runners from flat country usually find this harder than the climb — it is braking work for thighs and knees, and you will feel it on the stairs next morning.
The climb out (km 6.3–8.6)240 m up in 2.4 km, an average 10% with no flat sections: 40–50 minutes of continuous uphill, similar to climbing the stairs of the Dom Tower two and a half times, slowly. A steady pace and two short pauses do it. It is in shade the whole way.
UnderfootProper dirt footpaths: narrow, rooty, sometimes muddy. No scrambling, no exposure, nothing that needs hands. Trail-running shoes or light hiking shoes; not city trainers after rain.
No easy way outOnce you are at the bottom the only way back is up. There is no mobile signal in the canyon, so you cannot simply call a taxi. If in doubt, take the short version (page 3) or turn round at Van Wyck Meadow.

Who should think twice: anyone with knee trouble on descents, or who rarely walks more than an hour. Who will find it easy: anyone who hikes in hills a few times a year.

Step by step

Junctions are well signed with wooden posts giving trail names and distances in miles. Signs are the reason the trail names below matter: follow the names, not the arrows of other hikers.

DistanceWhat to do
10.0 km291 m Mountain Home trailhead, the car park across the road from the Inn. At the north end of the car park take the short flight of steps (the Trestle Trail) down to a narrow paved lane, Alice Eastwood Road. Turn right and follow it gently downhill.
20.8 km · 0.5 mi241 m Turn right onto the Troop 80 Trail, a level-to-rising footpath contouring through bay laurel, Douglas fir and the first redwoods, crossing several small streams on footbridges. After about 500 m the Sierra Trail crosses; keep straight on Troop 80. The road is above you but you will rarely notice it.
33.0 km · 1.9 mi314 m — high point Van Wyck Meadow, a small sunny clearing with a boulder and a hand-painted sign (“population: 3 Steller’s jays”). Good place for a snack. Turn left onto the Bootjack Trail, signed for Muir Woods, and start down. The trail follows Redwood Creek through an ever-deeper canyon, on steps, switchbacks and bridges; the redwoods get bigger as you drop. Ignore the spur to Alice Eastwood Road near the bottom.
45.1 km · 3.2 mi61 m The Bootjack Trail ends at the wide main trail of Muir Woods, close to Bridge 4. You are now in the National Monument. Keep left, heading downstream. Do not cross Bridge 4 (that leads to the Ben Johnson and Hillside trails).
55.4 km · 3.4 mi55 m The Fern Creek Trail comes in from the left (this is where the short version joins). Carry on down the main trail into Cathedral Grove, the heart of the old growth: trees up to 75 m tall and 600–800 years old. It is a designated quiet zone. Take your time here; this is what you came for.
66.3 km · 3.9 mi47 m — low point Just before Founders Grove, a signed trail with steps climbs away on the left: the Canopy View Trail. That is your way home. Optional: first continue 300 m down the main trail to the visitor centre for the café, toilets, water and to pay the entrance fee, then come back to this junction (adds 0.6 km).
Then climb. Canopy View rises steadily on switchbacks out of the redwoods into Douglas fir. No views despite the name, but deep quiet.
78.2 km · 5.1 mi220 m The Lost Trail joins from the left. Keep straight on, uphill, staying on Canopy View. The hardest part is behind you.
88.6 km · 5.4 mi285 m The trail tops out just below the Panoramic Highway. Turn left onto the Panoramic Trail, which runs level beside the road with views over the canyon you just climbed out of, towards the Pacific. After 330 m it meets Alice Eastwood Road: follow the lane and the Trestle steps back up to the car park.
19.3 km · 5.8 mi291 m Back at the trailhead. Cross the road to the Inn’s deck.

The short version — 6.4 km (4.0 mi), about 260 m of climbing, 2–2½ hours

Same redwoods, same climb out, but you skip Troop 80, the meadow and the Bootjack canyon. At the bottom of the Trestle steps turn left instead of right, pick up the Panoramic Trail (0.4 km) and turn right down the Canopy View Trail to point 7 (1.1 km). There turn right onto the Lost Trail, which drops 150 m in 800 m on a long series of steps — short, but the steepest thing in this guide. At the bottom turn left on the Fern Creek Trail and follow the creek 600 m to the main trail at point 5 (2.5 km). Continue as above from point 5.

Choose this if time or daylight is short, or if you would rather save your legs for the climb. Choose the main loop if you can: the Bootjack descent beside the creek is the most beautiful part of the day.

Practical

Getting to the start

Mountain Home Inn, 810 Panoramic Highway, Mill Valley. About 35 minutes from San Francisco: Highway 101 north over the Golden Gate, exit “Stinson Beach / Highway 1”, then follow signs for Mount Tamalpais onto Panoramic Highway. The public car park (free at the time of writing) is on the left, opposite the Inn. It is small and fills early on fine weekends: arrive before 9:00, or come on a weekday.

Without a car: Marin Transit route 61 (the West Marin Stagecoach) stops at the Inn, with a handful of trips a day from Marin City and Mill Valley. Check marintransit.org/routes/61.

When to go

Any time of year. Muir Woods is open from 8:00 until about sunset (17:00 in winter, as late as 20:00 in summer), and Mount Tamalpais State Park closes at sunset too. In winter start by 11:00 at the latest; 9:00 is ideal, and the grove is at its quietest before 10:00. Fog is common and suits the redwoods. November to March is the rainy season: expect mud, a louder creek, and from December perhaps spawning salmon in it.

Fees

Walking in is allowed, and no parking reservation is needed if you do not park at Muir Woods. The entrance fee still applies: US$15 per person aged 16 and over, payable at the visitor centre (point 6 detour). A US national-parks pass covers it.

Bring

  • 1 litre of water each. Taps at the trailhead and the Muir Woods visitor centre; nothing in between.
  • Shoes with grip, and a warm layer: the canyon floor can be 8–10 °C cooler than the ridge.
  • The route downloaded for offline use, and a payment card.
  • A snack for Van Wyck Meadow. Note that picnicking is not allowed inside Muir Woods itself.

Good to know

  • No mobile signal in Muir Woods or most of the canyon.
  • Toilets: at the trailhead and at the visitor centre.
  • No dogs, bikes or drones on these trails. Stay on the path — the redwood roots are shallow.
  • Poison oak grows beside the trails: a shrub with glossy leaves in threes, red in autumn. Touching it gives an itchy rash a day later. Just do not brush against the greenery.
  • Check for ticks afterwards. Rattlesnakes and mountain lions live here but are very rarely seen.
  • Car break-ins happen at trailheads. Leave nothing visible in the car — not even a jacket.
  • Emergency: 911. Park rangers: +1 415 388 2070.

Afterwards: where to eat

At the finish — Mountain Home Inn. The whole point of ending here: a wooden deck on the ridge looking over Mill Valley, the bay and, on a clear day, the East Bay hills. Hikers have been stopping here for over a century. Food is casual (one recent report says counter service rather than waiters) and opening hours vary by season, so check before you count on lunch: +1 415 381 9000, mtnhomeinn.com. Thursday is “locals night”, 17:00–20:00, with live music.

A day hike in two lengths · Mount Tamalpais, Marin County, California

Up the ladder from Stinson Beach

Leave the sand, climb a redwood canyon by stone steps, footbridges and one wooden ladder beside a waterfall, come down an open ridge with the Pacific in front of you, and walk from the end of the trail to a dinner table.

{{PHOTO:ladder}}

Why this hike

Steep Ravine is the best-loved trail on the mountain, and most people meet it from the top, parking at Pantoll and going down first. Starting at the beach turns the day the right way up: the climb comes while you are fresh, and the hike ends in a village with food.

  • The ladder. Halfway up the canyon the trail meets a wall of rock beside a waterfall and goes straight up it on 14 redwood rungs. Coming from the beach, you climb it.
  • A canyon of redwoods. Webb Creek runs all year under redwoods and ferns, and the trail crosses it again and again on wooden bridges.
  • The ocean on the way down. The Matt Davis Trail comes out onto open grass {{u:480 m|1,600 ft}} above the sea, then drops to the village with Stinson Beach, Bolinas Lagoon and Point Reyes ahead of you.
  • Dinner where the trail ends. The last signpost is two minutes on foot from a café with oysters on the menu and a bar with a burger (page 6). Order while your legs are still humming, then watch the sun go into the sea.

Two lengths. Turn round at Pantoll for a half-day loop, or carry on to East Peak, the summit of the mountain, for a full day from sea level to the top and back.

Is this for me?

The loop suits anyone who can hike steadily uphill for an hour and a half and is happy on a ladder. The peak version is a long day for people who already hike long days.

The loopAbout the effort of a brisk 17 km walk on the flat. The climb is 445 m from the beach to Pantoll, the height of a 120-storey tower, spread over 5 km and nearly all in shade. If you can jog {{u:5 km|5 km}} or cycle for two hours you have the engine for it.
The ladderAbout {{u:3 m|10 ft}} high, fixed to the rock, with side rails. It takes both hands and steady feet, the rungs are wet in winter, and the trail has no way round it.
The descent445 m down in 3.4 km on switchbacks and log steps: an hour of braking that flat-ground legs feel the next morning. Poles help.
The peakTwice the distance and twice the climb. The extra ground is gentle, on an old coach road and a railway grade, so the question is whether you have nine hours of hiking in you, and enough daylight (page 4).
Ways outPantoll has a bus stop and a ranger kiosk. From there the bus runs down to Stinson Beach a few times a day (page 5).

Why this way round

The ladder is climbed. Going up, you face the rungs and see your feet. Going down means backing over the edge onto wet wood.

Shade for the work. The climb is under redwoods beside cold water.

The sea for the reward. On Matt Davis the view is in front of you all the way down, and the low sun is on it.

On to the peak

From Pantoll the mountain rises another 335 m to East Peak, and the way up is gentle. It follows the old coach road and then the bed of the mountain railway, past the inn where the two once met. Until 1930 passengers rode the train up from Mill Valley to West Point and took a stagecoach down to Stinson Beach. You are hiking their journey the other way.

When to start, working back from dinner

Mount Tamalpais State Park closes at sunset, so be off the mountain by then: about 5 pm in December, 7 pm in March and September, 8:30 pm in June.

The loop, winterStart by 11:30. On the ridge about 3, at the beach for sunset, dinner at 5:30.
The loop, summerStart about 1:30. On the ridge in the long evening light, dinner at 6:30, sunset from the sand afterwards.
The peakStart by 8:00, March to October only. Summit about 12:30, Pantoll again about 3:30, dinner at 6.

Shorter days from the same car park

To the ladder and back. Hike to 3, climb it, and return the way you came, taking the ladder slowly on the way down: 7.4 km, about 300 m of climbing, 2½ hours.

Bus down from Pantoll. Hike up to 4 and ride route 61 back to the village (page 5): 4.9 km, all of the climb, none of the descent. Check the time of the bus before you leave the beach.

Without a car

Marin Transit route 61, the West Marin Stagecoach, runs from Sausalito and Marin City over the mountain to Stinson Beach every day, stopping at Pantoll on the way. At weekends the first bus leaves the Sausalito ferry terminal at 8:05 and reaches Stinson Beach at 9:12. The last bus back leaves Stinson Beach at 6:47 pm at weekends and 5:45 pm on weekdays (timetable of April 2026), so by bus dinner is an early one. Check the current timetable before you go.

Driving notes

Highway 1 is narrow and slow, with cyclists and sheer drops, and it is sometimes closed by slides after winter storms. The other way in is over the mountain: at D turn right on Panoramic Highway and follow it past Pantoll down to Stinson Beach. It takes about the same time. Returning to San Francisco, the bridge toll is collected southbound by number-plate camera; a rental company will bill it to you.

Stinson Beach, CA 94970. Allow 45–50 minutes from the Golden Gate Bridge and an hour from central San Francisco, more on a sunny weekend.

Parking, with dinner in mind

The beach car park (National Park Service) is free. Its gates open at 9:00 and close at a time posted at the entrance, which changes with the season, and cars left inside are ticketed. On hot days it fills before noon.

If dinner will run past the gate time, or you are starting before 9:00 for the peak, park on the street in the village, along Highway 1 or the side streets, obeying the signs. Any car should be fine on these roads; all of them are paved.

Afterwards: dinner at the end of the trail

This is the half of the day that the Pantoll car park cannot give you. The Matt Davis Trail lets you out on a village street, salt in the air, with four hours of hill in your legs and nothing left to do but sit down. Stinson Beach has one main street, and both of its restaurants are used to people arriving in boots.

Kitchens here close early. Sit down by 7 pm and you have your pick; arrive at 8:30 and the choice has narrowed to the snack bar or the market. If the sun is still up when you finish, carry a coffee across the car park to the sand. The beach faces west.

Practical

When to go

All year. Winter and spring are the best: the creek is loud, the falls beside the ladder are running and the ridge is green. Go a day or two after rain, when the steps have drained. Summer is dry underfoot and often foggy at the beach, with the ridge in sun above it. Autumn has the clearest air and the warmest evenings.

Bring

  • 1.5 litres of water each for the loop, 3 litres for the peak. Taps at the beach car park, West Point Inn and East Peak. At Pantoll, water came only from the ranger kiosk, about 9 am to 3 pm, when this was checked.
  • Shoes with grip, a wind layer for the ridge, and a headtorch if you start after noon in winter.
  • The route downloaded for offline use, and a card for dinner.

Good to know

  • Check the trail is open. Steep Ravine has been closed for months at a time after storms, most recently in summer 2025, to repair the ladder, bridges and a retaining wall. The park’s page lists closures; the kiosk is on +1 415 388 2070.
  • Toilets: beach car park, Pantoll, West Point Inn, East Peak.
  • No dogs or bikes on these trails.
  • Poison oak lines the Dipsea and Matt Davis trails: a shrub with glossy leaves in threes, red in autumn. Touching it gives an itchy rash a day later.
  • Check for ticks after the grassy stretches.
  • No mobile signal in the ravine; patchy elsewhere.
  • Leave nothing visible in the car. Emergency: 911.

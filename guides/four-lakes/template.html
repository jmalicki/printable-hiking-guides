A three-day backpacking loop · Trinity Alps Wilderness, northern California

A lake behind every ridge:
the Four Lakes Loop

Cross one high pass and the trail circles a single peak, handing you a new lake each time you top a ridge: four in {{u:eleven kilometres|seven miles}}, in rock that turns from rust-red to white as you go round. You pay for it on day one.

{{PHOTO:diamond}}

Why this hike

Most visitors to the Trinity Alps hike up Canyon Creek, see two lakes and hike back the same way. This trip costs one harder day and gives far more.

  • Four lakes, four rooms. Summit, Diamond, Luella and Deer Lakes each sit in their own ice-carved bowl on a different side of Siligo Peak. Every saddle opens a new view.
  • Two ranges in one. The trail crosses between rust-red rock and pale granite, with the white wall of Sawtooth Mountain filling the west (page 4).
  • No route-finding, no shuttle. Signed trail all the way, and you finish where you parked.

Is this for me? Honestly

The hiking is straightforward. The first day is not: 1,370 m of climbing with a full pack, most of it in the first 9 km.

FitnessDay 1 climbs the height of three Empire State Buildings, carrying a tent and three days of food. Running and cycling on flat ground give you the engine for it, but not the legs for 1,270 m of descent on the way out.
SkillOrdinary mountain hiking. Rocky, narrow and steep in places, and built across loose boulder slopes above Deer Lake. No scrambling.
RemotenessNo phone signal and no hut. From the far side of the loop, the car is most of a day away.
Easier optionCamp two nights at Deer Lake or Siligo Meadows and hike the loop with a day pack (page 3).
Two ways inThis guide follows Long Canyon, the shorter one. Stoney Ridge is 6 km longer, crosses two extra passes, and is described beside it on page 4.

Before you go, and when to turn back

Turn back if hard snow covers the traverse above Deer Lake (between 3 and 4). It faces north, holds snow well into July in a normal year, and a slip there runs out a long way. Go down to Deer Lake instead and visit the other lakes from that side. Also turn back if thunder is building before an exposed saddle. Ask the Weaverville ranger station about snow before you drive up: +1 530 623 2121.

The road to the Stoney Ridge trailhead may be rough: 10 km of dirt, officially fit for ordinary cars but with mixed reports (page 5). Long Canyon, the approach this guide follows, is an easy drive in any car.

Season and rules

  • Season: mid-July to early October in a normal year. Earlier, expect snow on the traverse above Deer Lake. Check for smoke and closures the day before.
  • Permit: a free wilderness permit is required to camp. Self-issue outside the Weaverville ranger station (360 Main St) on your way through. No quota.
  • Food storage: black bears live here. A bear canister is compulsory in some valleys (Canyon Creek for one); I could not confirm Deer Creek. Bring one.
  • Fires: assume no wood fires at the lakes, and none at all in late summer. A gas stove needs a free California Campfire Permit (permit.preventwildfiresca.org).
  • Groups of at most 10. Camp and wash 60 m from water. Carry out all rubbish.

The loop, clockwise

11.3 km and 690 m of climbing from the pass back to the pass, including Summit Lake
{{DETAIL}}

Why clockwise

Going this way the big views are in front of you on each descent: over Deer Lake from the traverse, across to Sawtooth as you drop to Diamond Lake, and down the valley from above Luella. It also puts the one snow-prone slope first, while you can still change your plan cheaply.

Where to sleep

Summit Lake is the high camp, with the longest evening light. Deer Lake and Siligo Meadows (just south of the pass, with water) are the usual base camps. Diamond and Luella are small and open; I found no reliable account of pitches there, so do not count on one. Use existing bare sites, well back from the shore.

The loop as a day hike

From a camp at Deer Lake the full circuit is the same 11.3 km with a light pack: 4–5 hours. Shortest taste: pass to Summit Lake and back, 4.6 km, which still crosses the slope above Deer Lake.

Getting up there: two ways in

Described in this guide

Long Canyon · 9.0 km to the pass, +1,260 m

The shortest way to the loop, and steep with it: the trail gains height almost without a break. Allow 4–5 hours up with a full pack and 2½–3 down. Choose it for the lakes, or if you have only two days.

1Long Canyon trailhead, 1,150 m. Fill in a wilderness permit if there is a box at the board. The trail climbs through pine and fir forest above the creek. Carry water from the start in late summer.
2The meadows, 5.7 km, 1,890 m. The forest opens into steep green meadows under the cliffs of Gibson Peak, with flowers into July and established camp spots. This is the best place to break the climb.
3Deer Creek Pass, 9.0 km, 2,305 m. Switchbacks lead to Bee Tree Gap at the head of the canyon. The trail then crosses the top of Siligo Meadows to the pass.
The longer alternative

Stoney Ridge · 12.1 km to the pass, +1,230 m

Starts 260 m higher and climbs more gently, but crosses two passes before the third, and you re-cross both on the way out (+340 m on the last day). Choose it if you have four days and want the high meadows, or a night at Echo Lake. Its access road is 10 km of dirt with mixed reports (page 5).

Stoney Ridge trailhead, 1,410 m. The first {{u:kilometre|half mile}} or so crosses logged private forest before the wilderness boundary. Then come 57 switchbacks in older forest. The creek is close but below the trail; there is a small spring at Red Mountain Meadow.
BStonewall Pass, 7.3 km, 2,245 m. The first wide view, over Van Matre Meadows to the central peaks. The trail skirts the meadows and crosses the stream from Echo Lake.
CLittle Stonewall Pass, 9.7 km, 2,260 m. A narrow gap between red cliffs. Drop into Siligo Meadows (ponds, water, good camping) and climb to 3 at 12.1 km.

Why the lakes do not match: red rock, white rock and ice

From Deer Creek Pass the rock around you is the colour of rust. Across the valley to the west, Sawtooth Mountain is nearly white. Locals call these the Red Trinities and the White Trinities; this loop crosses the join several times in a day.

The red rock is peridotite, a heavy, dark-green rock from the Earth’s mantle, pushed up when pieces of ocean floor were welded onto North America. Its iron rusts in the air. Plants struggle on it, so the red slopes are open and thinly wooded.

The white rock is granite, molten rock that rose into the older rocks and cooled underground. It weathers into clean slabs and carries denser forest. Siligo Peak itself is a granite top standing among red neighbours.

{{PHOTO:redwhite}}

Ice did the rest. Each lake lies in a cirque, an armchair-shaped hollow dug by a small glacier at the head of its own valley. Four glaciers on four sides of one peak left four bowls with thin walls between. That is why two lakes 1 km apart on the map are a 150 m climb apart on foot, and why the north-facing bowl above Deer Lake keeps its snow longest.

Water

Where it is

  • Long Canyon. Small streams on the lower switchbacks, then the creek through the meadows. Fill at the top of the meadows: there is nothing over Bee Tree Gap until the springs in Siligo Meadows. I found no late-season report, so in September carry enough for the climb.
  • Stoney Ridge. The creek is rarely reachable from the switchbacks, and in a dry year the side streams had gone by late June. First dependable water: the spring at Red Mountain Meadow, 8 km and 690 m up.
  • The loop. Every lake, a spring below Diamond Lake, and Deer Creek. No gap is longer than two hours.

How clean, and what to do to it

  • Clear, with bits in it. The lakes are small and shallow, with tadpoles by September, and people swim in Summit Lake, which has no stream in or out. Harmless once filtered. Draw from springs, inlets and outlets where you can.
  • Treat all of it. Giardia is present in most of the wilderness, the Forest Service says. A filter, tablets or a UV pen is enough.
  • Upstream. Pack horses graze the meadows. I found no report of cattle, and nothing against water off the red rock.
  • Burns. The 2025 Peak Fire closed both approach trails until January 2026. Ask about the Stoney Ridge side.

Getting to the trailheads

Bring

  • Paper topographic map and compass, plus the GPX on your phone. There is no signal.
  • 3 litres of water capacity each and a filter (page 4).
  • An early start: both approaches begin low and face the sun. Be walking by 7:00 on day 1.
  • Sun hat and sunscreen (the red slopes have almost no shade), a warm layer and waterproof (nights at 2,200 m can be near freezing in August), and poles for the descents.

From San Francisco allow 5½–6 hours to Weaverville: Interstate 5 north to Redding (350 km), then Highway 299 west for 70 km, a winding mountain road.

Road warning · rated for cars, reports mixed · 10 km of dirtThe Forest Service keeps this road up for ordinary cars (level 3), but drivers disagree: some call it fine, others rough and rocky. Gravel at first, bare dirt for the last 2 km. Expect ruts, drive slowly, and take the vehicle with more clearance if you have a choice. Single lane with unsigned forks, and phone maps misroute, so follow the signs. Open 1 May to 31 October. Long Canyon is the easier drive.

Leave nothing in view in the car, and no food at all: this is bear country.

Without a car

Not practical. Trinity Transit buses link Redding and Weaverville, but nothing runs up Highway 3 to either trailhead.

Staying and eating

Weaverville is a well-kept gold-rush town with motels and places to eat; The Nugget (790 Main St) is the classic diner for the meal afterwards. To start early, camp the night before at one of the Forest Service campgrounds along Highway 3 on Trinity Lake.

# Printable hiking guides

Paper guides to hikes worth the drive: five or six pages you can print, fold into a pocket and follow where a
phone has no signal.

## The idea

Most trail information is either an app that needs signal and battery, or a write-up that sells the hike without
telling you whether you can do it. Each guide here tries to do five things on a few sheets of paper:

- **Make the case for the hike**: why this one, against the obvious alternative.
- **Say honestly who it is for.** Every guide has an "Is this for me?" section: distance, climb, time, and what is
  actually hard about it.
- **Put everything in one place**: a topographic map, an elevation profile, step-by-step directions keyed to the
  map, how to get to the trailhead, and where to eat afterwards.
- **Explain what you are looking at**, where you are standing: the geology, the history, the plants and animals.
- **Warn early.** Snow slopes, rough roads, dry stretches and old mine workings are on page 1, not page 5.

They are written for a fit visitor who does not know the area. They are also honest about their own limits: most
were compiled from maps, land-manager pages and published trip reports and were not walked for the guide, and each
one says so. Check conditions and closures with the land manager before you go.

Everything is built by scripts from open data (OpenStreetMap trails, USGS maps and elevation), so a guide can be
corrected and rebuilt, and new ones added the same way.

## The guides

- **[Bay Area hikes](BAY-AREA-HIKES.md)**: ten day hikes around San Francisco Bay, from Point Reyes, Mount Tamalpais
  and the San Mateo coast to the Ohlone Wilderness.
- **[Further afield](FURTHER-AFIELD.md)**: the redwood coast, the northern Sierra, the central coast, the Trinity Alps,
  Yosemite, Yellowstone and Grand Teton, including multi-day backpacking trips.
- **[Extremely hazardous advanced routes, documented for reference](HAZARDOUS-ROUTES.md)**: routes that
  experienced backpackers advise against, compiled from trip reports and never walked for these guides. They are
  here to show what such a route involves.

The finished PDFs are in this repository, under `out/<guide>/`, in two editions: US Letter in feet and miles
(`<name>.pdf`) and A4 in metric (`<name>-metric-a4.pdf`). Each folder also has GPX tracks to load into CalTopo,
Gaia, Komoot or AllTrails.

## Printing a guide

A guide is meant to get rained on. Checked in November 2026; shop offerings and prices change.

**Settings, whatever you print on**
- Use the Letter edition in the US and the A4 edition elsewhere.
- Print at **100% / "actual size"**, not "fit to page", in colour, double-sided. The maps have a drawn scale bar,
  which stays correct at any size, but shrinking makes the map labels harder to read.

**Best: a plastic sheet from a print shop.** This is the most water-resistant way to print a guide you can
still fold and put in a pocket. The sheet is polyester with no wood fibre in it, so it has nothing to soak up
water: it comes out of a wet pocket or a puddle as it went in, and it is very hard to tear. Toner is fused onto the
surface and stays there when wet.

- **FedEx Office** (formerly Kinko's) sells it under the misleading name **"water-resistant paper"**. It is a
  7.7 mil synthetic sheet, tear-resistant and grease-proof, in Letter and 11 × 17 in, printed in full colour on
  both sides. FedEx lists field guides and field manuals among its uses.
  - At the counter: "full colour, double-sided, letter size, on your water-resistant synthetic paper, 7.7 mil."
  - Online: *Copies & Custom Documents*, upload the PDF, and choose the water-resistant stock.
  - Phone the shop first to check it has the stock that day. The self-service machines take standard paper
    only, and FedEx does not let customers load their own.
  - FedEx describes it as good for rain, spills and quick dips, and stops short of calling it waterproof for
    long soaking. It recommends scoring the sheet before folding, so ask them to score it or fold it once,
    firmly, where you want the crease.
  - Price: ask. The only figure I found is a government contract rate of $0.92 a double-sided colour sheet
    (May 2026), so expect a few dollars for a whole guide.
- **Why it beats coated paper.** "Water-resistant" and "all-weather" papers such as standard Rite in the Rain
  are wood-pulp paper with a coating. They shed rain well, and the maker's own comparison sheet separates them
  from its synthetic stock, which it rates for use under water. Coated paper can still tear, and a hiker in
  coastal Alaska reports that it wets through in the end where the synthetic absorbs nothing.
- **What people who work in the wet say.**
  - Philip Tschersich, hiking in coastal Alaska, wrote on Backpacking Light that the synthetic sheet takes up
    no water and that he would not go back to a zip bag
    ([2015](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/104121/)).
  - John Godino of Alpinesavvy calls a plastic sheet close to tear-proof and says it takes colour laser
    printing very well ([Alpinesavvy](https://www.alpinesavvy.com/blog/use-waterproof-paper-for-maps)).
  - Orienteering New Zealand reported that maps on a synthetic sheet kept their ink at the folds through a wet
    four-hour event ([2020](https://www.orienteering.org.nz/blog/waterproof-paper-discussion-june-2020/)).
  - Orienteering Australia notes that putting paper maps in bags adds cost and work and makes them harder to
    read ([Orienteering Australia](https://orienteering.asn.au/printing-orienteering-maps/)).
  - Commercial trail maps are printed this way: Green Trails moved its maps onto a waterproof, tear-resistant
    synthetic ([Washington Trails Association, 2014](https://www.wta.org/news/signpost/reliable-green-trails-maps-get-a-refresh)).
- **The other view.** Andrew Skurka prints maps on ordinary paper at FedEx Office, carries them in a gallon
  zip bag, and judged waterproof stock not worth the extra cost for his mostly dry trips
  ([andrewskurka.com](https://andrewskurka.com/printing-exporting-topographical-maps-from-digital-sources/)).
  For a dry-season day hike that is a fair call.
- **Lamination** seals a page completely, which makes it the one thing more waterproof than the plastic
  sheet, but it turns each page into a stiff card that should stay flat. It suits a single page such as the
  route map.
- **By post:** Doxzoo prints single copies from a PDF on synthetic sheets in both Letter and A4.

**Cheapest: plain paper in a bag.** Print on ordinary paper and carry it folded in a gallon zip-top freezer bag,
map page outwards. Laser prints cope with damp better than inkjet prints: most inkjet colour inks are dye-based
and run when wet. Paper of any kind tears once it is soggy.

**At home**
- **Laser printer:** buy the same kind of synthetic sheet (Revlar, iGage Weatherproof, TerraSlate) and print
  on both sides. Check that your printer's manual allows synthetic or label stock: a few fusers run too hot
  or too cool for it. Rite in the Rain all-weather printer paper (Letter and A4) is the coated-paper
  alternative, cheaper and less durable.
- **Inkjet printer:** most waterproof stocks are made for toner and will not hold ordinary inkjet ink. Papers
  such as PuffinPaper and iGage Weatherproof are sold for *pigment*-ink inkjets (and lasers); dye inks run. If
  you are not sure which you have, print on plain paper and bag it, or let a shop print it.

## Building and editing

Each guide is a folder under `guides/` holding a `config.py` (route, maps, photos) and a `template.html` (the
words). `out/<guide>/` gets the PDFs, GPX files, `guide.html` and page previews. Style, audience, required sections
and working method for writing a new guide are in `AGENTS.md`: read it first. After adding or changing a guide, run
`scripts/make_index.py` to refresh the three lists above (set `REGION='bay-area'` in the config for a Bay Area hike, `REGION='hazardous'` for a
route documented as advised against, `LISTED=False` to keep a guide out of the lists). A guide to a hike the author has not done carries a red
banner on page 1; see `AGENTS.md`.

## Build
- `make` — rebuild every PDF from cached data (no network)
- `make UNITS=metric PAPER=a4` — the metric A4 variant, written as `<name>-metric-a4.pdf` (defaults: `UNITS=imperial PAPER=letter`). Templates are written in metric and converted at build time; `{{u:metric text|imperial text}}` overrides the conversion
- `make GUIDE=west-point` — one guide
- `make routes-west-point` — recompute a guide's route + GPX from cached trail data
- `scripts/lint_guide.py west-point` — list layout and wording findings for a built guide (`--all` for every guide); a short form runs after every build
- `make refresh GUIDE=west-point` — re-download trails, basemaps, driving route and photos, then rebuild

## Where to edit
- **Words**: `guides/<slug>/template.html` (placeholders: `{{u:metric|imperial}} {{MAP}} {{DETAIL}} {{PROFILE:route}} {{PROFILE}} {{ACCESS}} {{PHOTO:key}} {{PHOTO_CREDITS}}`)
- **Route, map labels, profile notes, driving-map markers, photos**: `guides/<slug>/config.py`
- **Shared look**: `guides/shared.css`
- **Finding photos**: `.venv/bin/python scripts/commons_search.py "query"` then add the `File:` title to `PHOTOS` in the config; or a Flickr CC photo by direct URL with `artist`, `license`, `page`

## Off-trail legs
`scripts/dem_route.py <slug>` samples a terrain grid (config `DEM`) and traces a least-steep line between the waypoints in `OFFTRAIL`; `build_routes.py` splices those legs in and marks them so maps draw them dashed. The traced line is an approximation for the map, not a surveyed track. A guide can also have a close-up map (`DETAIL`, `DETAIL_TILES`, `{{DETAIL}}`) and extra driving routes (`ACCESS['extra']`).

## Work in progress
A guide that is not ready, or not meant to be published, lives in `wip/<slug>/` (`config.py`, `template.html`, and its own
`data/` and `out/` inside that folder). `wip/` is in `.gitignore`. Every script takes the slug as usual
(`make GUIDE=<slug>`, `scripts/build_routes.py <slug>`); the hike lists, credits and `lint_guide.py --all` leave `wip/` out.
To publish one, move `config.py` and `template.html` to `guides/<slug>/`, `data` to `data/<slug>/` and `out` to `out/<slug>/`.

## New guide
Copy a guide folder, edit `config.py` (bounding boxes, junctions, route order), then
`scripts/fetch.py <slug>`, `scripts/build_routes.py <slug>` (prints leg-by-leg trail names to check), write the template, `make GUIDE=<slug>`.

Needs python3, Google Chrome (headless PDF), venv with pillow + pymupdf (`make .venv`).
Data: trails (c) OpenStreetMap contributors; basemap USGS The National Map; elevation USGS 3DEP via opentopodata.org; driving route OSRM; photos Wikimedia Commons and Flickr (credited in each guide).
